How this little garden teaches

Original home practice for Eric: he knows letters and is beginning to blend. Researched October 25, 2026.

Reinforce the approach at school

Hamden’s language arts overview describes Fundations, Heggerty, Wit & Wisdom and Geodes. The Connecticut district list confirms that combination. This is district-level alignment, not knowledge of Eric’s current classroom unit or an official district app. The words, tales, illustrations and scripts here are original.

Sounds connect to print

The IES practice guide supports explicit sound-to-letter work, decoding and daily connected reading. The game uses these principles:

  1. See the letters from the start, and tap to hear each sound.
  2. Tap the sounds from left to right; blend them into a whole word.
  3. Build a word with one sound in each box. Two letters such as sh can share a box.
  4. Look at every letter when choosing between similar printed words. Pictures do not supply the answer.
  5. Read a short sentence, then enjoy language and meaning in a story.

The full IES guide advises minimizing a strong added “uh” after consonants. Prepared phoneme clips model sounds separately from letter names. Short vowels come first; a later digraph path is enabled by a grown-up.

Stories to listen to, and little turns to read

The three six-page tales have richer listening text and separate simple child reading turns. The whole story is not labeled beginner-decodable. Spoken word timestamps drive the highlight, and a tapped word can replay its part of the narration. A question and a retell invitation keep meaning in view.

Encouragement without pressure

There are no lost lives, speed tests or countdowns. Leaves recognize completed practice. Hints are welcome. Records show practice and matches without an answer hint; they do not measure reading age or prove independent reading.

The game cannot hear or judge pronunciation. A grown-up can listen and compare Eric’s attempts with his teacher’s models. The small mouth drawings are reminders, not precise articulation diagrams or speech therapy.

Prepared audio, private play

Directions, words and stories use a prepared US Google voice with word timestamps. Isolated sounds use explicit US phonemes prepared locally with eSpeak NG, cut before any carrier vowel. Google bare-consonant clips were replaced after checks found letter-name substitutions. The sound models have a more synthetic timbre than the narrator. Automated checks do not replace an adult listening alongside Eric. Playing loads saved files from the family server. There is no microphone, live model call, ad, tracker or purchase. Progress stays in the browser. This prototype has not been independently studied as a reading intervention.
